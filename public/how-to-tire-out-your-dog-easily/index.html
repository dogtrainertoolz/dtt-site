How to Tire Out Your Dog: Brain Games and Better Walks

Mental exercise tires dogs out faster than running. Easy games and walk upgrades for a calmer, happier dog.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

"A tired dog is a good dog," but the best kind of tired comes from using their brain and nose, not just their legs. Physical exercise alone can build a fitter dog who needs even more.

Upgrade your walks

  • Sniff walks: let your dog lead the pace and sniff on a long leash. Ten minutes of sniffing is hard mental work.
  • Change routes often so there are new smells.
  • Add training moments: sits at crossings, a few heel steps, recall games on a long line.

Food games

  • Scatter feeding: toss part of a meal in the grass and let your dog sniff it out.
  • Snuffle mats and puzzle feeders Snuffle mats · Puzzle toys
  • Stuffed, frozen food toys for longer sessions Stuffable toys
  • Lick mats: calming and absorbing Lick mats

Brain games at home

  • Find it: hide treats or a toy around the house.
  • Which hand? Hide a treat in one fist and let your dog choose.
  • Cup game: hide a treat under one of three cups and shuffle.
  • Name your toys: teach your dog to fetch toys by name.
  • New tricks: see 7 fun tricks.

Active play

  • Tug (with rules: drop it on cue, and play stops if teeth touch skin)
  • Flirt pole for chase-loving dogs, in short bursts Flirt poles
  • Fetch, in moderation. Endless ball throwing can over-excite some dogs and strain joints.

How much is enough?

It depends on breed, age and health. Watch your dog: a well-exercised dog settles calmly at home and sleeps well. Restless chewing, barking and zoomies usually mean they need more variety, not just more running.

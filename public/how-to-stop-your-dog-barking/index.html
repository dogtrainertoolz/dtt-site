How to Stop Your Dog Barking Too Much

Barking is communication. Figure out why your dog is barking, then use the plan that fits that reason.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

You'll never get a dog to stop barking completely, and you shouldn't want to. But excessive barking usually has a cause you can work on. Start by working out what kind of barking it is.

1. Alert barking (doorbell, windows, passers-by)

  • Manage the view: frosted window film, closed curtains or blocking access to the front window. Window privacy film
  • Teach "thank you": when your dog barks, say "thank you" calmly, then call them to you and reward. You've acknowledged the alert and given them something to do instead.
  • Teach a "go to mat" for the doorbell: ring it, toss a treat on the mat, repeat until the bell means "run to my mat."

2. Demand barking (at you, for food, play or attention)

  • Don't reward it. Turn away or leave the room until there's a few seconds of quiet, then give attention.
  • Get ahead of it: reward quiet, settled behavior before the barking starts.
  • Meet the need on your schedule: play, walks and meals at predictable times.

3. Boredom barking

  • More exercise, sniffing walks and brain games
  • Food puzzles and chews when you're busy or out

4. Fear or reactive barking

Barking at dogs or people on walks is usually fear or frustration. See leash reactivity.

5. Barking when left alone

If your dog barks, howls or whines only when alone, see separation anxiety.

What to avoid

  • Yelling. To your dog, it sounds like you're barking along.
  • Bark collars that shock, spray or vibrate. They punish the barking without addressing why your dog is barking, and can increase fear or stress.
  • Debarking surgery. It doesn't address the cause and is widely opposed by veterinary organizations.

When to see your vet

Sudden new barking, especially at night or in an older dog, can be a sign of pain, hearing or vision loss, or cognitive changes.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.

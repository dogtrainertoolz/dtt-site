How to Potty Train Your Puppy: A Step-by-Step Plan

A simple routine of frequent breaks, rewards and supervision that gets most puppies reliably house-trained, without punishment.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Potty training is mostly about timing and management. Your job is to get your puppy outside before they need to go, reward them for going in the right spot, and prevent accidents indoors while their bladder control develops.

Use our free potty schedule generator to build a schedule around your puppy's age and your day.

The routine

Take your puppy outside:

  • First thing in the morning and last thing at night
  • Within 10–15 minutes after every meal
  • Right after waking from a nap
  • After play, chewing sessions or excitement
  • Every hour or so for young puppies (under about 12 weeks), and stretching longer as they grow
  • Any time they start sniffing the floor, circling or wandering off

Step by step

  1. Go to the same spot every time. Carry young puppies so they don't have an accident on the way.
  2. Stand quietly and wait. Use a calm cue like "go potty" as they start to go.
  3. Reward the moment they finish: a tasty treat and happy praise, right there outside. Not when you get back inside, which is too late.
  4. Then a little play or a short walk. If you always go straight back in, some puppies learn to hold it to stay outside longer.
  5. Nothing after 5 minutes? Go back in, keep your puppy in their crate or close to you for 10–15 minutes, then try again.

Indoors: supervise or confine

Most accidents happen when nobody's watching. When you can't actively supervise, use a crate, pen or puppy-proofed small room. When your puppy is loose, keep them in sight, or tethered to you with a leash.

See crate training.

When accidents happen

  • Don't punish. Rubbing noses in it or scolding teaches puppies to hide when they go, not to go outside.
  • If you catch them in the act, calmly interrupt ("oops!"), carry them outside and reward if they finish there.
  • Clean with an enzyme cleaner. Ordinary cleaners leave a scent that draws puppies back. Enzyme cleaners

How long does it take?

Most puppies are reliable somewhere between 4 and 6 months, but some take longer, especially small breeds. Progress isn't always steady; a few setbacks are normal.

See your vet if your puppy has frequent accidents despite a good routine, strains to pee, pees very small amounts often, or seems to drink much more than usual. A urinary infection or other medical issue may be involved.

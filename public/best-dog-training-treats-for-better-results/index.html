The Best Treats for Dog Training

What makes a good training treat, how to choose the right value for the situation and how to keep calories under control.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

In reward-based training, treats are your paycheck. The right treat at the right moment makes training faster and more fun.

What makes a good training treat

  • Small: pea-sized or smaller, so your dog can eat it in a second and you can give lots.
  • Soft: quick to eat, with no crumbs to sniff out.
  • Smelly and tasty: especially outdoors.
  • Easy to carry in a pocket or treat pouch.

Low, medium and high value

Value Examples Use for
Low Kibble, plain dry treats Easy skills at home
Medium Soft commercial training treats Most training sessions
High Small pieces of chicken, cheese, hot dog, freeze-dried liver Recall, distractions, reactivity, scary things

Match the treat to how hard the task is for your dog right now.

Keep calories in check

Treats should be no more than about 10% of daily calories. Use our free treat calorie calculator to see your dog's budget. Tips:

  • Break treats into tiny pieces.
  • Use part of your dog's daily food as rewards, especially at home, and feed a little less at meals.
  • Check the bag: some biscuits and chews are 30+ calories each.

Foods to avoid

Never use grapes or raisins, onion, garlic, chocolate, macadamia nuts, or anything with xylitol (some peanut butters and sugar-free products). Check labels.

Not just food

Toys, tug, a game of chase, sniffing time and access to things your dog wants are all powerful rewards. Mixing them keeps training interesting.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.

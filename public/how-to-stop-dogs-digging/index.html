How to Stop Your Dog Digging Up the Yard

Digging is a natural behavior. Find out why your dog digs, then give them a better place to do it.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Many dogs love to dig, especially terriers, hounds and Huskies. You'll have more success redirecting digging than trying to stop it entirely.

Why dogs dig

  • Boredom or excess energy
  • Cooling off: digging a cool spot in hot weather
  • Hunting: chasing moles, voles or insects underground
  • Burying bones and toys
  • Escape: digging under fences, often driven by boredom, mating instincts or anxiety
  • Fun! For some breeds, it's simply what they were bred to do

The plan

  1. Give your dog a dig zone. A sandbox or a marked corner of the yard with loose soil. Bury toys and treats there to make it exciting, and praise when your dog digs in it. Dog sandboxes
  2. Make the wrong spots unappealing. Fill holes, cover favorite spots with rocks or chicken wire just under the surface, or block access with fencing.
  3. Supervise yard time while you're working on it, and calmly redirect to the dig zone.
  4. Meet the need behind it: - More exercise and sniffing walks - Food puzzles and brain games - Shade and a cooling mat in hot weather - Deal with burrowing pests safely and humanely
  5. Don't leave your dog alone outside for long periods. Boredom digging thrives there.

Escape digging

If your dog digs under the fence, bury chicken wire or pavers along the fence line, and look at why they want out. If it happens mainly when they're alone, consider separation anxiety.

Lawn safety

Keep your dog away from freshly treated lawns, fertilizer, slug bait and cocoa mulch, which are all toxic to dogs.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.

How to Teach Your Dog to Walk to Heel

Heel means walking right at your side, focused on you. A fun, reward-based way to teach it for busy streets and crowds.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

"Heel" is more precise than loose-leash walking: your dog walks right at your side (usually the left), matching your pace. It's useful for crowded sidewalks, crossings and passing other dogs. You don't need it for the whole walk; your dog should still get time to sniff.

Step 1: Find the position

  1. Stand still with your dog on your left. Hold treats in your left hand.
  2. Lure your dog so they're standing beside your left leg, facing forward.
  3. Mark "yes!" and reward right at your leg. Repeat until your dog seeks out that spot.

Step 2: Add movement

  1. Say "heel" and take one step. Reward at your leg if your dog stays beside you.
  2. Build to two steps, three, five, ten.
  3. Reward often at first, every step or two, then gradually less often.

Step 3: Turns and pace changes

Practice turns, slow walking, fast walking and stops. Reward when your dog keeps up. Turning away from your dog (right turns) and towards them (left turns) both help them pay attention.

Step 4: Take it outside

Start in a quiet driveway, then a quiet street, then busier places. Lower your expectations each time the environment gets harder.

Release breaks

Say a release word like "free" and let your dog sniff on a loose leash. Switching between heel and free time keeps walks enjoyable and makes heel easier to hold.

Tips

  • Keep sessions short: a minute or two of heel at a time to start.
  • Use high-value treats outdoors.
  • Don't drag your dog into position. Lure and reward instead.
  • If your dog lunges at dogs, people or traffic, work on reactivity separately.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.

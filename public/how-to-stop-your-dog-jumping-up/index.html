How to Stop Your Dog Jumping Up on People

Dogs jump to greet and get attention. Teach an alternative they can do instead, and make sure jumping never pays.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Dogs jump up because it works: they get closer to faces, and people react, even if it's to push them away. The fix is to make jumping pointless and give your dog a better way to say hello.

1. Teach an alternative greeting

Choose one: sit, four paws on the floor or go to a mat.

  1. Practice with family first. Approach your dog calmly.
  2. Before they jump, ask for a sit (or reward four paws on the floor).
  3. Reward low, near the floor, and greet calmly.
  4. If they start to jump, turn away and step back. Try again.

2. Make jumping unrewarding

  • Turn away and fold your arms. No eye contact, no talking, no pushing.
  • Greet only when four paws are on the floor.
  • Everyone must do the same. One person who lets the dog jump "because they don't mind" keeps the habit alive.

3. Manage visitors

  • Keep your dog on a leash when guests arrive, or behind a gate until they settle.
  • Toss treats on the floor as people come in. Dogs can't jump while sniffing for food.
  • Ask guests to ignore your dog until they're calm, then greet them low and slow.

4. Lower the excitement

  • Keep your own arrivals home low-key for a few minutes.
  • Give your dog exercise before visitors come.
  • Practice greetings often, so they become less of a big event.

What not to do

Don't knee your dog in the chest, step on their toes or shout. It can hurt them, and it still gives them attention.

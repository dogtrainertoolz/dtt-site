How to Stop Your Dog Pulling on the Leash

Dogs pull because pulling works. A step-by-step loose-leash plan, plus the gear that helps (and the gear to avoid).

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Dogs pull for a simple reason: it gets them where they want to go, faster. To fix it, pulling has to stop working, and walking nicely beside you has to pay off.

The right gear

  • A front-clip harness reduces pulling without hurting your dog. The leash clips at the chest, so pulling turns your dog sideways. Front-clip harnesses
  • A 6-foot flat leash. Avoid retractable leashes while training; they teach dogs that pulling extends the leash.
  • A treat pouch and plenty of small, tasty treats. Treat pouches

Avoid prong, choke and shock collars. They can cause pain, injury and fear, and studies link them to more behavior problems. Reward-based training works as well or better.

Step 1: Teach the "sweet spot" indoors

  1. With your dog on leash in a quiet room, take a step. When your dog is beside your leg, mark "yes!" and give a treat right at your side.
  2. Keep walking in small circles, rewarding often for being next to you.
  3. Gradually take more steps between treats.

Step 2: Stop when the leash gets tight

On walks:

  1. The moment the leash goes tight, stop. Stand still like a tree.
  2. Wait until your dog turns back or the leash loosens.
  3. Mark and reward at your side, then continue walking.

Step 3: Change direction

If your dog keeps pulling, cheerfully say "this way!", turn and walk the other way. Reward when they catch up beside you.

Make it easier to succeed

  • Exercise first. A quick game in the garden takes the edge off.
  • Let your dog sniff on cue. Sniffing is the best part of a walk for a dog. Say "go sniff" and let them explore on a loose leash, then call them back.
  • Start in boring places before busy streets.
  • Be consistent. If pulling works sometimes, your dog will keep trying.

How long does it take?

Expect weeks, not days, especially with an adult dog who's pulled for years. Short, frequent practice beats long frustrating walks.

Want your dog right at your side? See walking to heel. Lunging and barking at dogs or people is a different problem: see reactivity.

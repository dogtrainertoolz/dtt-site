Off-leash time lets dogs run, sniff and explore in ways a leash can't match. But it's only safe and fair to others when your dog has the skills and the place is right.

## Is your dog ready?

Your dog is likely ready when they:

- **Come back reliably** when called, even with mild distractions, practiced on a long line (see [recall](/how-to-teach-your-dog-recall/))
- **Check in with you** regularly without being asked
- **Leave it** reliably for food and interesting smells
- **Don't chase** wildlife, joggers, bikes or cars
- **Are friendly or calmly indifferent** to other dogs and people

If any of these aren't solid yet, keep using a long line. It gives your dog freedom while you keep control.

## Is the place right?

- **Check the rules.** Leash laws are common, and many parks and beaches allow off-leash dogs only in designated areas.
- **Avoid roads,** livestock, nesting wildlife areas and busy paths.
- **Be careful in fenced dog parks** if your dog is nervous, very small or rough with others.
- **Respect others.** Not everyone loves dogs, and some dogs on leash are reactive or recovering from surgery. Call your dog back before they approach.

## Building up

1. **Long line in open spaces.** Practice recalls and check-ins.
2. **Drop the line** and let it drag, so you can step on it if needed.
3. **Short off-leash periods** in safe, quiet spaces, with lots of recalls and rewards.
4. **Gradually more time** and more varied places.

## Safety

- **ID tags and a microchip** with up-to-date contact details.
- **Consider a GPS tracker** for dogs who roam or bolt. [GPS dog trackers](amzn:gps+dog+tracker)
- **Know your dog's limits.** Scent hounds and sighthounds may never be reliable off leash near open land, and that's okay.

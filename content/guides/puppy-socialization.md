Socialization means giving your puppy **positive** experiences with the people, animals, places, sounds and handling they'll meet in life. Puppies who miss out are more likely to grow into fearful or reactive adults.

**Track your progress with our free [puppy socialization checklist](/tools/puppy-socialization-checklist/).**

## The sensitive period

Puppies accept new things most easily from about **3 to 14 weeks of age**. After that, new experiences are more likely to cause fear. That's why it matters to start as soon as you bring your puppy home.

## But what about vaccines?

The window overlaps with the time when your puppy isn't fully vaccinated. Veterinary behavior experts recommend socializing anyway, safely, because behavior problems are a leading cause of dogs being given up. Ask your vet about your puppy's specific risks, and:

- **Carry your puppy** in public places, or use a stroller or sling.
- **Avoid places where unknown dogs gather,** like dog parks and pet store floors, until your vet says it's okay.
- **Meet healthy, vaccinated adult dogs** you know.
- **Join a puppy class** that requires vaccinations and keeps play supervised.

## How to do it well

- **Keep it positive.** Pair every new experience with treats and calm praise.
- **Start at a distance** and let your puppy watch. Get closer only if they're relaxed.
- **Watch body language.** Tail tucked, ears back, freezing, hiding or trying to leave means it's too much. Move away and try again another day.
- **Short and often** beats long and overwhelming.
- **Never force an interaction.** Let your puppy choose to approach.

## Don't forget

- **Handling:** paws, ears, mouth, brushing and nail trims.
- **Being alone:** short periods in a crate or pen, so your puppy learns that alone is okay.
- **Sounds:** vacuum, traffic, doorbells and low-volume recordings of fireworks and thunder.

Socialization doesn't stop at 14 weeks. Keep giving good experiences through adolescence.

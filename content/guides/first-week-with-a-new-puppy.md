The first week sets the tone. Puppies thrive on routine, sleep and gentle introductions to their new world.

## Before your puppy comes home

- **Puppy-proof:** hide cords, shoes and anything chewable; lock away medicines, cleaners and trash.
- **Set up a puppy zone:** a crate and an exercise pen or gated area with a bed, water and toys. [Puppy playpens](amzn:puppy+playpen+indoor)
- **Supplies:** food the breeder or shelter used, bowls, collar with ID tag, harness, leash, enzyme cleaner, chew toys and treats.
- **Book a vet visit** for the first week.
- **Agree on house rules** with everyone at home: words for cues, sofa or not, who feeds when.

## Day 1

Keep it calm. Show your puppy their potty spot first, then their puppy zone. Limit visitors. Expect some crying the first night. Keep the crate by your bed so you can reassure your puppy and hear when they need to go out. See [crate training](/how-to-crate-train-a-puppy/).

## Days 2–3: routine

- Meals at the same times
- Frequent potty breaks (use the [potty schedule generator](/tools/potty-schedule-generator/))
- Short play sessions followed by naps. Puppies need a lot of sleep.
- Start handling paws and ears with treats

## Days 4–7: start training and socializing

- **Name game:** say your puppy's name, reward when they look at you.
- **Sit** and **come** in short, fun sessions. See [training basics](/dog-training-101-how-to-teach-the-basics/).
- **Short alone time** in the pen or crate with a chew.
- **Gentle socialization:** new sounds, surfaces and calm visitors. See the [socialization checklist](/tools/puppy-socialization-checklist/).

## Common first-week mistakes

- Too much excitement and too little sleep
- Too much freedom too soon, which leads to accidents and chewed shoes
- Long training sessions (3–5 minutes is plenty)
- Punishing accidents or biting

Be patient. Most puppies settle into the new routine within a couple of weeks.

A reliable "down" helps your dog settle in cafés, at the vet and when visitors arrive. It's also the start of [stay](/how-to-teach-your-dog-to-stay/) and "settle on a mat."

## Method 1: Luring

1. **Start with your dog sitting.**
2. **Hold a treat at their nose,** then slowly move it straight down to the floor between their front paws.
3. **Pause there.** Many dogs fold down. Mark with "yes!" and reward as their elbows touch the floor.
4. **If they stand up,** start again and move the treat more slowly. You can also try moving it down and then slowly away from them along the floor, in an L shape.
5. **Repeat 5–10 times**, then fade the treat from your hand and use the hand signal alone.
6. **Add the word "down"** once they're following the signal reliably.

## Method 2: Capturing

Some dogs, especially small or anxious ones, don't like being lured down. Instead:

1. **Sit somewhere quiet with treats nearby.**
2. **Wait until your dog lies down on their own,** then mark and reward.
3. **Toss the treat a little away** so they get up, and wait for the next down.
4. **Add the word** once they're offering it often.

## Tips

- **Use a soft surface.** Many dogs don't like lying on cold or slippery floors.
- **Teach it when your dog is a little tired,** not wired.
- **Don't push your dog down.** Pressure makes many dogs resist.
- **Reward in position:** give the treat between their paws while they're still down.

## Next steps

Add duration: wait a second, then two, before rewarding. Then build a [stay](/how-to-teach-your-dog-to-stay/).

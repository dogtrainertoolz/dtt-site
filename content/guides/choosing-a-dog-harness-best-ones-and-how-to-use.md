The right gear makes training easier and safer. The wrong gear can hurt your dog or make problems worse.

## Collars

A **flat collar** with an ID tag is a must for identification. For leash walking, though, many dogs are better off with a harness, especially pullers, small dogs and flat-faced breeds, since pressure on the neck can injure the windpipe.

**Martingale collars** tighten slightly so a dog can't back out of them. They're useful for sighthounds with narrow heads. Fit them so they don't tighten fully.

## Harnesses

- **Front-clip harnesses** clip at the chest and reduce pulling by turning your dog sideways. Great for training loose-leash walking. [Front-clip harnesses](amzn:front+clip+no+pull+dog+harness)
- **Back-clip harnesses** are comfortable for dogs who already walk nicely, but they can encourage pulling.
- **Dual-clip harnesses** give you both options.

**Fit:** two fingers under the straps, no rubbing behind the front legs and a Y shape across the chest that doesn't restrict the shoulders.

## Head halters

Head halters (like a horse halter) give strong control of large or powerful dogs. They need careful introduction with treats, and you should never jerk the leash while one is on.

## Leashes

- **A 6-foot flat leash** for everyday walking and training.
- **A long line (15–30 feet)** for recall practice and safe freedom. [Long lines](amzn:long+line+dog+training+leash+30+ft)
- **Avoid retractable leashes** while training. They teach pulling, give poor control and can cause injuries.

## Gear to avoid

**Shock (e-collars), prong and choke collars** work by causing discomfort or pain. Research links them to stress, fear and aggression, and reward-based training is just as effective. Many veterinary and behavior organizations recommend against them.

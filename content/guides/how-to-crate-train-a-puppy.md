A crate gives your puppy a safe, cozy place to rest, helps with potty training and keeps them out of trouble when you can't watch them. The goal is a puppy who walks in on their own and settles happily, not one who's shut in and panicking.

## Setting up

- **Size:** big enough to stand up, turn around and lie stretched out. For a growing puppy, buy an adult-size crate with a divider and expand the space as they grow. [Crates with dividers](amzn:dog+crate+with+divider)
- **Location:** somewhere near the family, not in a far-off room. At night, many puppies settle best with the crate next to your bed at first.
- **Comfort:** a washable bed or blanket and a safe chew toy. Cover the crate with a light cloth if your puppy likes a den feel.

## Step by step

1. **Make it a treat zone.** Leave the door open and toss treats inside. Let your puppy go in and out freely.
2. **Feed meals in the crate**, with the door open at first.
3. **Close the door for a few seconds** while they eat, then open it before they finish.
4. **Build time slowly:** close the door with a long-lasting chew, sit next to the crate, then move around the room, then leave the room briefly.
5. **Practice when your puppy is tired,** after play and a potty break, so settling is easy.
6. **Gradually increase the time** your puppy spends in the crate while you're home, before using it when you go out.

## Crying in the crate

- **First, check needs.** Has your puppy had a potty break, exercise and something to chew?
- **Young puppies at night:** if your puppy wakes and cries, they may need to go. Take them out calmly, with no play, and straight back to the crate.
- **Wait for a pause** before opening the door during the day, so you reward quiet, not crying.
- **Go slower.** Lots of crying means the steps were too big. Go back a step.
- **Real panic** (drooling, frantic escape attempts, injuring themselves) isn't normal protest. Stop, and see [separation anxiety](/how-do-i-stop-my-dog-getting-separation-anxiety/).

## How long is too long?

Puppies can't hold their bladder for long. Use the [potty schedule generator](/tools/potty-schedule-generator/) as a guide. Even adult dogs shouldn't spend most of the day crated. If you're out all day, arrange a midday walker or visit.

**Never use the crate as punishment.** It should always be a good place to be.

**"Leave it"** means "don't touch that." **"Drop it"** means "let go of what's in your mouth." Both are about trading: your dog learns that giving something up gets them something even better.

## Teaching "leave it"

1. **Put a treat in your closed fist** and hold it near your dog's nose. Let them sniff, lick and paw. Say nothing.
2. **The moment they back off,** even for a second, mark "yes!" and give a **different, better** treat from your other hand.
3. **Repeat** until your dog backs away from your fist right away. Then add the words "leave it" just before you present your fist.
4. **Open hand:** show the treat on your open palm. If your dog goes for it, close your hand. Reward from the other hand when they look away.
5. **On the floor:** put a treat on the floor under your hand or foot. Reward for leaving it. Gradually uncover it.
6. **Real life:** practice on walks with "planted" items, then dropped food in the kitchen.

Never let your dog get the "leave it" item. The reward always comes from you.

## Teaching "drop it"

1. **Play tug with a toy.** Stop moving the toy, hold it still and put a tasty treat right at your dog's nose.
2. **When they let go,** say "yes!", give the treat, then give them the toy back and continue playing.
3. **Add the words "drop it"** just before showing the treat.
4. **Practice often with toys,** so letting go never means losing the fun.

## If your dog grabs something dangerous

Stay calm. Don't chase, which turns it into a game. Offer a high-value trade or toss a few treats on the floor away from the item, then pick it up. If your dog may have swallowed something toxic, call your vet right away.

If your dog growls, freezes or snaps when you approach their food or toys, see [resource guarding](/how-to-stop-resource-guarding/) and get professional help.

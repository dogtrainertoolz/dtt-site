A great recall isn't about obedience. It's about making coming to you **more rewarding than anything else** going on. That takes practice, generosity and never letting "come" mean something bad.

## The rules

1. **Coming to you always pays.** Use your best treats: chicken, cheese, hot dog pieces. [High-value training treats](amzn:high+value+dog+training+treats)
2. **Never call your dog for something unpleasant:** baths, nail trims, ending fun, or being told off. Go and get them instead.
3. **Don't call if you can't make it happen.** Every ignored recall teaches your dog the word is optional.
4. **Don't chase.** Run away from your dog instead; most dogs follow.

## Step by step

1. **Charge the word.** In a quiet room, say "come" (or a whistle) and immediately give a treat. Repeat 10 times. You're building a happy association.
2. **Short distances indoors.** Say your cue when your dog is a few feet away. Reward and praise big when they reach you, and touch their collar as you treat so being caught is part of the game.
3. **Recall games:** two people take turns calling the dog back and forth across a room or garden.
4. **Outdoors on a long line.** Use a 15–30 foot line in a park or field so your dog can't practice running off. [Long training lines](amzn:long+line+dog+training+leash+30+ft)
5. **Add distractions slowly:** call when your dog is mildly interested in something, not when they're chasing a squirrel.
6. **Release back to fun.** Often, after rewarding a recall, let your dog go back to sniffing or playing. Coming back doesn't always mean the fun ends.

## Practice the emergency recall

Pick a special word or whistle for emergencies only and pair it with a jackpot: lots of treats, one after another. Practice it occasionally so it stays powerful.

## Common problems

- **Dog comes but stops just out of reach:** reward closer to your body, and practice collar touches with treats.
- **Ignores you at the park:** go back to the long line and easier distractions.
- **Great at home, terrible outside:** normal. Outside is much more exciting. Practice in lots of places.

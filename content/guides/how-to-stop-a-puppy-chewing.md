Dogs need to chew. Puppies chew to explore and soothe teething gums, and adult dogs chew to relieve boredom and stress. You can't stop chewing; you can direct it.

## 1. Manage the environment

- **Puppy-proof:** pick up shoes, socks, remote controls, kids' toys and anything you'd hate to lose.
- **Protect cables** with covers, or block access.
- **Use a crate, pen or gates** when you can't supervise.
- **Keep trash and laundry behind closed doors.**

## 2. Provide great chew options

Rotate a few types to keep them interesting:

- Rubber chew toys and food-stuffable toys, frozen for longer sessions [Stuffable chew toys](amzn:stuffable+rubber+dog+chew+toy)
- Edible chews sized for your dog, with supervision
- Frozen washcloths or teething toys for puppies
- Snuffle mats and puzzle feeders for mental work [Puzzle feeders](amzn:dog+puzzle+feeder)

**Avoid** cooked bones, antlers and very hard chews that can break teeth. A good rule: if you can't dent it with your fingernail, it's too hard.

## 3. Teach what to chew

When your dog picks up something they shouldn't:

1. **Don't chase.** It turns into a game.
2. **Trade:** offer a tasty treat or a toy, and praise when they drop the item. See [leave it and drop it](/teach-your-dog-to-leave-it/).
3. **Give an appropriate chew** right away.

Praise your dog whenever you see them chewing their own toys.

## 4. Meet their needs

A lot of destructive chewing happens when dogs are bored, under-exercised or anxious. Make sure your dog gets daily exercise, sniffing time and [brain games](/how-to-tire-out-your-dog-easily/).

If destruction happens only when your dog is alone, especially around doors and windows, it may be [separation anxiety](/how-do-i-stop-my-dog-getting-separation-anxiety/) rather than boredom.

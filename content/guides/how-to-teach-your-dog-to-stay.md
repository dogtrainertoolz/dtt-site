"Stay" means "keep doing what you're doing until I release you." It's one of the most useful safety skills you can teach, and one of the easiest to rush.

## Before you start

Your dog should know **sit** or **down**. Choose a **release word** like "okay" or "free" that means "you can get up now." Every stay ends with the release, so your dog learns it's you who decides when it's over.

## Step 1: Duration

1. Ask for a sit or down.
2. Say "stay" with a flat hand signal.
3. **Count one second,** then mark with "yes," reward and release.
4. Build slowly: 2 seconds, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30. Mix in easy ones.

## Step 2: Distance

Go back to short durations. Take **one step back,** return to your dog, reward, then release. Build to two steps, three, across the room, and around a corner. Always **return to your dog to reward** rather than calling them to you.

## Step 3: Distraction

Go back to short duration and close distance again. Add one small distraction: bend down, clap once, drop a glove, bounce a ball. Build slowly to bigger distractions, then try new places.

## The golden rule

**Change only one thing at a time.** If you add distance, keep the time short. If you go somewhere new, make it easy again.

## If your dog breaks the stay

No scolding. Calmly reset them in the starting position and make the next one easier. If they break often, you're moving too fast.

## Practice ideas

- A stay before meals, before going through doors and before getting out of the car
- Stay while you open the front door
- Stay on a mat while you eat dinner

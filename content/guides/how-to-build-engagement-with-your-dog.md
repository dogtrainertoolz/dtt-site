If your dog seems to ignore you, especially outside, the problem is usually that the world is more interesting than you are right now. Engagement training makes paying attention to you a habit that pays.

## The name game

1. In a quiet room, say your dog's name once in a happy voice.
2. **The instant they look at you,** say "yes!" and reward.
3. Repeat 10 times a session, a few sessions a day.
4. Move to slightly busier places: the garden, the driveway, a quiet street.

Never use your dog's name to scold them.

## Reward check-ins

Whenever your dog looks at you on their own, especially on walks, mark it and reward. You're teaching that checking in with you is a great idea. Soon your dog will offer it constantly.

## Make yourself interesting

- **Play** with a tug toy or a quick game of chase on walks.
- **Scatter a few treats** for your dog to sniff out.
- **Change direction** cheerfully and reward when they follow.
- **Hand-feed part of meals** during short training sessions to build a strong food-reward habit.

## Check the basics

Dogs struggle to listen when they're:

- **Over threshold:** too close to something exciting or scary. Move further away.
- **Under-exercised** or bored.
- **Confused:** the cue means different things to different people, or it's been repeated so often it's background noise.
- **Not motivated:** a dry biscuit can't compete with a squirrel. Use better rewards outdoors.
- **In pain or unwell:** a sudden change in responsiveness is worth a vet check.

## Practice ideas

- Five name-game reps before every walk
- Reward every voluntary check-in for a week, and watch them increase
- A 2-minute training game in each room of the house
